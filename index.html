      About the Model

      Sound

      The piano is synthesized in the browser with the Web Audio API: a two-operator FM tone for the chords and a bass built from its first four harmonics, so the bass pitch comes through on small speakers. It is not a recorded piano.

      Meter and rhythm

      Each chord lasts a whole number of beats, one bar unless you change it, and may have a rest before it. Nothing plays during a rest; the beat keeps counting. A chord's length is kept as a share of a bar, so when you change the meter a one-bar chord stays one bar and a half-bar chord stays half a bar, rounded to the nearest beat. The accompaniment pattern follows the meter's beats, and every chord change sounds its bass and chord together. Each loop starts again on beat 1, so if the chords do not fill the last bar, that bar is played short.

      Time is counted in eighth-note pulses. Simple meters (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4) split each beat into two pulses; compound meters (6/8, 9/8, 12/8) split each beat into three, and their tempo counts dotted quarter notes. 7/8 groups its pulses 2 + 2 + 3 and counts its tempo in quarter notes. The downbeat is played loudest, the meter's other strong beats next, and the pulses between beats softest.

      Voicing

      Smooth voicing searches the chord tones between G3 and A5 for the set that moves the upper voices the fewest total semitones from the previous chord, while keeping them close together. Block voicing stacks each chord upward from its root near middle C, with no voice leading.

      Tension and next-chord hints

      Tension values (0 to 100) are illustrative scores, not measurements. Each chord starts from a value for its function (tonic low, dominant high), and 7ths, suspensions and inversions add to it. The "often next" hints follow common-practice tendencies, such as V moving to I or vi; any chord can follow any other.

      Spelling and the circle of fifths

      Notes are spelled from the key's scale, so a key uses one letter per scale degree (F♯ in D major, G♭ in E♭ minor). On the circle, major keys sit on the outer ring and their relative minors on the inner ring. Your progression's roots are marked on their wedges; a diminished chord is marked on the minor wedge with the same root (B° on Bm). Show your progression turns the marks and arrows off and on.
